ENTRY 01
THE PROBLEM
status: well documented
(third-party data)
sourced

Industrial surfaces lose the fight against corrosion — constantly.

Corrosion and fouling degrade pipes, tanks, hulls and structural steel wherever moisture and industrial chemistry meet metal. The result is unplanned downtime, expensive maintenance cycles, and equipment replaced long before it should be.

ENTRY 02
THE HYPOTHESIS
status: grounded in external research
not yet tested in-house
external evidence only

Our hypothesis rests on real, published science — we haven't run our own experiments yet.

We're working from SLIPS — Slippery Liquid-Infused Porous Surfaces — first developed at Harvard's Wyss Institute in 2011, inspired by the Nepenthes pitcher plant, whose naturally slippery rim lets nothing keep its footing.

A porous surface is deliberately pre-filled with a liquid. Once every pore is occupied, water, oil, salt spray or biofilm simply have nowhere to grip — they slide off a continuous liquid film instead of bonding to a solid one.

Commercial proof this scales: LiquiGlide, an MIT spin-off, has taken the same underlying principle to market — from consumer packaging to its CleanTanX™ system for industrial tanks, oil & gas, and paint manufacturing, now serving 30+ paying industrial clients.

What's still ours to prove: whether our own formulation holds up on the substrates and conditions our target industries actually use. That's exactly what Entry 03 is about.
